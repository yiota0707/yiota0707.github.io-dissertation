How the model works

Learning, interaction and partner choice

Each agent learns from its previous interactions while the structure of the population determines who interacts next.

01

Agents are paired

Agents begin in pairs and repeatedly play the Prisoner's Dilemma with their current partner.

02

They cooperate or defect

Each action is selected from learned Q-values using Boltzmann exploration.

03

Rewards update learning

Interaction rewards influence the probability of choosing each action in future encounters.

04

Partners may change

The hybrid model learns partner switching while assortativity influences the rematching process.

Prisoner's Dilemma

Individual temptation versus collective benefit

Mutual cooperation rewards both agents. A defector can exploit a cooperator, while mutual defection leaves both agents with a lower reward.

Research questions

What creates stable cooperation?

The project investigates when cooperation emerges, how quickly it stabilises and whether partner-selection mechanisms help agents escape persistent defection.

Central comparison

Baseline versus hybrid partner selection

Both systems are evaluated under matched conditions so that the effects of learned partner switching and assortative rematching can be examined.
